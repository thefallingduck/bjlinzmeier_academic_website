If fossils, ancient oceans, or the history of life interest you, geology at the University of South Alabama is a good place to explore them. I'm Ben Linzmeier, an assistant professor in the [Department of Earth Sciences](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/). I use the chemistry of fossil and modern shells to understand the ecology of cephalopods and the environments they lived in, and I mentor undergraduates who want to do paleontology research.

## Can you study paleontology at the University of South Alabama?

Yes. I teach the paleontology course, and the department offers a [Bachelor of Science in Geology](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/geology/majoringeology.html), and paleontology is part of the geology core. The department's Geology page highlights small classes with individualized attention, field trips and research opportunities, and nearby Gulf Coast environments such as Dauphin Island, the Mobile-Tensaw Delta, and Mobile Bay.

**GY 401, Paleontology (W)**, which I teach and expect to offer next spring, is a 4-credit, writing-intensive course. The catalog describes it as "evolutionary, ecological, and environmental processes from the study of marine invertebrate fossil groups." Its prerequisites include EH 102 and introductory geology (GY 111, or GY 112 with GY 112L). See the [full catalog entry](https://bulletin.southalabama.edu/courses-az/gy/) for the exact requirements.

Introductory courses are a good place to start. In **Physical Geology (GY 111)** you learn about Earth's materials and the processes that shape the planet. In **Earth History (GY 112)**, sometimes called historical geology, you trace the history of the Earth as recorded in its rocks and the life they contain.

## Paleontology research with undergraduates

I mentor students on paleontology research projects. I'm open to many kinds of projects, and my goal is to help you build skills while you work on them. The department's Geology page says undergraduates may be invited to join faculty research.

## Where to start

**If you're in high school:** read about the [B.S. in Geology](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/geology/majoringeology.html), then [apply to the university](https://www.southalabama.edu/departments/admissions/applytousa.html) or [request information from the department](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/geology/contactus.html).

**If you're at a community college:** South Alabama's [Pathway USA](https://www.southalabama.edu/departments/admissions/pathwayusa) program works with community colleges including Bishop State Community College, Coastal Alabama Community College, Lurleen B. Wallace Community College, and Mississippi Gulf Coast Community College. The registrar's [Geology transfer guide](https://southalabama.edu/departments/registrar/areav/geology.html) lists the courses from Alabama community colleges that count toward a geology degree.

**If you're already at South Alabama:** if the fossil record or Earth's history in Physical Geology or Earth History has caught your attention, talk to me or to the department. The [Geology page](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/geology/majoringeology.html) links the change-of-major forms, and the [geology minor](https://www.southalabama.edu/colleges/artsandsci/earthsci/geology/minoringeology.html) is another option.

## Where a geology degree can lead

The department's Geology page lists paleontology among the fields where geology graduates work, along with environmental and engineering geology, petroleum and mineral exploration, hydrology, seismology, GIS and remote sensing, teaching, and government work. Many paleontology careers involve graduate study, and the department recommends a full year of calculus and physics for students planning graduate school. If you want to know what that path looks like, ask me.
